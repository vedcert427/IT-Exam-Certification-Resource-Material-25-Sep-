Study method

Certification Preparation Guide

Passing a certification exam is mostly a planning problem. The steps below describe a study method that works for technical and non-technical certifications alike.

01

Understand the exam objectives

Read the official objectives before you study anything else. Note the weight of each domain and the question formats, then build your entire plan around that outline instead of around a single textbook.

02

Create a realistic study schedule

Count the weeks until your exam date and the hours you truly have each week. Divide topics across that budget with one buffer week at the end, and schedule shorter review sessions in addition to new material.

03

Learn core concepts before memorization

Understand why a technology or process works before memorizing its details. Conceptual understanding survives scenario questions, while memorized lists often fail when the wording changes.

04

Study official documentation

Vendor documentation and official study guides describe the world the exam is written from. Note default values, named procedures, and terminology exactly as the documentation uses them.

05

Build practical understanding

Use free tiers, trial tenants, sandboxes, or simulators wherever possible. Performing a task once teaches more than reading about it three times and makes scenario questions far easier.

06

Practice questions responsibly

Use practice questions to find gaps, not as a substitute for learning. For every answer you get wrong, study the underlying topic until you can explain why the correct option is correct.

07

Review weak areas deliberately

Keep a running list of topics you miss or avoid. Schedule specific sessions for that list instead of rereading material you already know, and close entries only when you can teach them.

08

Take timed practice sessions

At least twice before exam day, sit a full-length mock under real time limits with no notes. Pace problems are much easier to fix in practice than in the testing center.

09

Track preparation progress

Check off objectives as you cover them and record your mock scores over time. A simple spreadsheet is enough to show whether your plan is working and where to spend the remaining weeks.

10

Revisit difficult topics

Hard topics need several passes separated by days. Space your revisits so each round tests recall first and only then consults notes, which builds much stronger memory.

11

Final exam preparation

Use the last week for light review, logistics, and rest. Confirm your ID and exam rules, plan your route or testing setup, and avoid cramming new material the day before.
